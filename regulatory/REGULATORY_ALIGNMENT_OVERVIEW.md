# Regulatory Alignment Overview  
Version 0.1 — Public, Non-Normative  
Status: Informational Reference Document  

## Purpose

This document provides a high-level overview of how the Conscious Protocol (CP) aligns with existing and emerging regulatory frameworks across key jurisdictions.

It is intended to support:
- public-sector review,
- regulatory sandbox participation,
- conformity assessment discussions, and
- internal evaluation by standards and oversight bodies.

This document is non-normative. Normative requirements are defined in the relevant protocol specifications and compliance documents.

---

## Governance Context

The Conscious Protocol is stewarded by an independent, non-profit foundation currently in formation (the “Foundation”).  
The Foundation is responsible for:
- protocol governance,
- versioning,
- public-interest stewardship.

WOKE GLOBAL is a commercial entity that develops implementations compatible with the protocol.  
WOKE GLOBAL does not control protocol governance and does not unilaterally define compliance criteria.

## 1. European Union Alignment

### Regulatory Context

The European Union has established a risk-based framework for artificial intelligence systems, particularly through the AI Act, GDPR, and associated guidance.

High-risk AI systems are expected to demonstrate:
- lawful processing of sensitive data,
- meaningful human oversight,
- auditability and traceability,
- proportional risk mitigation.

### Conscious Protocol Alignment

The Conscious Protocol is designed to support these objectives by defining minimum technical conditions for systems that interact with, infer, or respond to human cognitive or affective states.

Key alignment points include:

- **Consent Semantics**  
  Explicit, revocable consent is treated as a stateful process rather than a one-time event.

- **Auditability**  
  State transitions related to consent, activation, suspension, or override are designed to be loggable and reviewable.

- **Risk Minimisation**  
  The protocol focuses on minimum viable safety guarantees rather than optimisation or behavioural manipulation.

- **Separation of Roles**  
  Clear separation between protocol governance (Foundation) and commercial implementations (vendors).

The protocol is intended to be compatible with conformity assessment processes under the EU AI Act for relevant risk categories.

## 2. Asia-Pacific Alignment

### Regional Context

Several Asia-Pacific jurisdictions emphasise:
- operational trust,
- social stability,
- deployable governance mechanisms rather than abstract principles.

Frameworks in this region often adopt or adapt externally validated standards once they demonstrate regulatory maturity.

### Alignment Approach

The Conscious Protocol is positioned as:
- jurisdiction-neutral,
- compatible with public-sector certification frameworks,
- focused on implementation clarity rather than normative ethics.

Key characteristics relevant to Asia-Pacific adoption include:

- **Implementation-Focused Design**  
  Clear technical affordances rather than interpretive guidelines.

- **Human-Centric Safety Framing**  
  Emphasis on preventing unintended cognitive or psychological impact.

- **Compatibility with Certification Models**  
  Designed to map to checklist-based assurance frameworks rather than discretionary review.

This makes the protocol suitable for integration into regional AI assurance or verification programs without modification of core semantics.

## 3. United States Alignment

### Regulatory Context

The United States employs a sectoral approach to AI oversight, with enforcement driven by:
- healthcare regulation,
- consumer protection,
- liability and insurance frameworks,
- judicial interpretation of “reasonable practice.”

### Alignment Characteristics

The Conscious Protocol is designed to support defensible system operation by:

- providing evidence of consent handling,
- enabling post-hoc audit and review,
- establishing clear boundaries between system capability and human agency.

While not a regulatory requirement, the protocol may serve as evidence of reasonable and proportionate safeguards in regulated sectors such as healthcare, digital therapeutics, or human-facing AI services.

## Cross-Jurisdictional Properties

Across all regions, the protocol intentionally prioritises:

- backward compatibility,
- conservative evolution,
- minimal required surface area,
- transparency over optimisation.

The protocol is not designed to:
- maximise engagement,
- infer mental states without consent,
- replace clinical or legal judgment.
